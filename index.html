About

I am a PhD student in Environmental Engineering at UFPR, advised by Prof. Nelson Luís Dias, specializing in micrometeorology and atmospheric turbulence. My work bridges experimental and numerical domains: I study atmosphere-forest interactions, develop novel spectral analysis methods, and write custom Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) solvers. To drive these models, I focus on high-performance scientific programming using Chapel and Python for numerical methods and CPU/GPU parallel computing. Additionally, my background includes previous experience in coastal wave modeling.

Ongoing Projects

A Spectral Formulation Based on the Geometric Mean: Development and Application to Turbulent Flow Analysis

This research introduces a novel spectral formulation utilizing the geometric mean, generalized through a logarithmic-modulus approach. The primary goal is to resolve the sign alternation issues frequently encountered in co-spectra—especially at high frequencies—without sacrificing physical interpretability. By applying this methodology to atmospheric turbulence data, this project aims to significantly enhance the robustness and reliability of spectral analysis under real-world observational constraints.

The Influence of Topography and Vegetation on Turbulence in the Amazon Forest Roughness Sublayer

This project investigates the complex dynamics of turbulence within the Amazon rainforest canopy at the Amazon Tall Tower Observatory (ATTO). Focusing on the roughness sublayer, we explore how local topography and dense vegetation modulate airflow. Using multi-height micrometeorological data (35m, 50m, and 81m), the research analyzes the turbulent kinetic energy (TKE) and temperature variance budgets to provide a more accurate characterization of atmosphere-forest interactions.

In-Situ Calibration of Sonic Anemometer Temperature Bias Using the Temperature Variance Budget

This project develops an innovative analytical method to correct temperature biases in sonic anemometers using the atmospheric temperature variance budget. By transforming an ill-conditioned optimization problem into an exact linear system, this approach mathematically preserves the physical integrity of the turbulent temperature gradient. The methodology provides a robust QA/QC tool for correcting multi-level micrometeorological data, even in the absence of absolute temperature references.

High-Performance Direct Numerical Simulation (DNS) of Scalar Transport in Rayleigh-Bénard Convection

This project involves the development of a highly optimized 2D Direct Numerical Simulation (DNS) solver written in the Chapel programming language, designed to investigate passive and active scalar transport in Rayleigh-Bénard Convection. By employing advanced numerical schemes—including a TVD Superbee limiter and a Multigrid V-Cycle Poisson solver—the code accurately resolves scalar micro-filaments with extreme Schmidt numbers (up to Sc=100) without artificial diffusion.
